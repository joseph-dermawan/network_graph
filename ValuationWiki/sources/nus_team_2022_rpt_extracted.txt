4. Investment Summary
Thesis 1: Buying opportunity before excessive COVID-19 pessimism reverses
Prior to the onset of COVID-19, SATS traded between the $4.80 - $5.20 range in 2H2019. Following the
introduction of market volatility owing to COVID-19 concerns, SATS fell below this range and closed at
a 5-year-low of $2.58 on 23rd Mar 2020. Ever since, SATS has yet to recover to its previous high and its
share price continue to be troubled by COVID-19 developments. We believe that these worries are
unwarranted, and SATS is a value play as air travel is overdue for recovery.
Capitalising on recovery potential of travel-related stocks with capped downside: From our
analysis of the largest market-cap stocks on SGX [Figure 4.1], the three travel-related stocks (SATS,
Singapore Airlines and Genting Singapore) rank in the bottom 6 in terms of COVID-19 share price
recovery. As the other sectors have mostly recovered while travel has not, this suggests the market’s
severe pessimistic outlook around travel-related stocks. On the contrary, we believe that Omicron has
allowed air travel to achieve an inflection point due to the mounting scientific evidence regarding the
ineffectiveness of travel restrictions. This is aligned with IATA’s call for governments to accelerate

to remove entirely or scale back restrictions serves as further evidence. In Singapore, the renewed

SATS is well-positioned to weather the COVID-19 storm: As we arrive at the 2-year mark of COVID-

Appendix K: Further Investment Risks for Consideration
Business Operations Risks
B3 | Food supply chain disruptions – The pandemic has placed unprecedented stresses on food supply chains, with disruptions to labour, processing, transport, and logistics. While adequate
response has been made when food supply chain has been compromised, higher raw materials could arise in the event of continued disruptions. This would in turn compress SATS’ profit
margins if the company is unable to pass on the costs to customers.
B4 | Technological risk – Despite the development and adoption of new technological innovations, it still remains to be seen if the investments will yield fruits. If SATS is unable to effectively
increase its value added per capita, decrease costs, dominate both the food solutions and gateway services markets, and successfully launch into new market segments, investors are exposed to
the downside.
Financial Risks
F1 | Foreign currency risk – While SATS operates principally in Singapore and generates revenue mainly in SGD, SATS also has investments outside of Singapore and it operates in more than
13 countries, hence it is exposed to the effects of fluctuations in certain FX rates because of its foreign currency dominated operating revenue and expenses. For example, a reasonably possible
strengthening (weakening) of the Singapore dollar, would increase (decrease) profit or loss by a fair amount. However, the overall impact of FX rate fluctuations on the Group’s operations are
relatively low as the Group’s sales and purchases are mainly denominated in the respective functional currencies of the Group’s entities.
F2 | Interest rate risk – SATS’ earnings are affected by changes in interest rates due to the impact that such changes have on its interest income from cash and cash equivalents and its interest
expense on term loans. SATS’ interest-bearing assets and interest-bearing liabilities are predominantly denominated in SGD, JPY, and USD. Changes in market interest rates for these currencies
could significantly affect the interest income or finance charges of variable interest financial instruments. Moreover, changes in market interest rates could also affect the carrying value of
financial instruments with fixed interest rates if these are recognised at their face value.
