## 2. Core Variant Theses

### Thesis 1: The Euphoria Purge Settles at DCF Gravitational Parity
During the first half of 2026, market participants bid Micron to $1,255, pricing in a permanent High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) supply deficit and indefinitely high unit margins (>70%). As hyperscaler CapEx growth normalized and competitors expanded capacity, the multiple compressed. However, fundamental intrinsic value did not collapse because Micron **already banked $59.0B in FY2026 free cash flow**, fortifying its balance sheet to $73.45B in liquid assets. At $594.51, the stock trades within 1.6% of our $604.08 DCF intrinsic value and within 0.6% of the Monte Carlo median ($590.70).

### Thesis 2: HBM Base Die & Packaging Barrier Prevents Rapid Commoditization
Unlike legacy commodity planar DRAM, HBM3E and HBM4 architectures require 18–24 month TSMC CoWoS advanced packaging qualification, thermal substrate matching, and direct co-design with Nvidia, Broadcom, and AMD. Micron's high-margin HBM capacity is 100% committed through calendar 2026 under signed Long-Term Agreements (LTAs), insulating cash flows in Years 1–3 from spot memory price fluctuations.

### Thesis 3: Structural Oligopoly Discipline Supports 35% Long-Run Operating Margin
In prior cycles (2001, 2008), 15+ memory manufacturers engaged in suicidal price wars, driving industry margins deeply negative. Following decades of consolidation, the global DRAM market is partitioned into a disciplined 3-player oligopoly: Samsung (~41%), SK Hynix (~32%), and Micron (~25%). Because cleanroom CapEx costs exceed $20B per mega-fab, rational supply discipline ensures that long-term normalized EBIT margins settle at **35.0%**, well above legacy commodity baselines.
