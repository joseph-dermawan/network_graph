## 1. Executive Summary
The **Capital Cycle Framework**, pioneered by Marathon Asset Management and documented by Edward Chancellor in *Capital Returns*, explains how capital allocation drives long-term industry profitability and asset returns. 

In traditional valuation, analysts linearly extrapolate high current margins and earnings growth into the future. The Capital Cycle demonstrates that **capital is reflexive**: abnormal profitability acts as a beacon that attracts massive capital expenditures, leading to industry overcapacity, price competition, and subsequent collapse in Return on Invested Capital ($ROIC$).

```

                │        THE CLASSIC CAPITAL CYCLE          │
```

## 2. Key Differences in the AI Supercycle

In traditional industrial cycles (steel, shipping, semiconductors, telecom), the spenders and suppliers are often in distinct commercial spheres, or capital is fragmented. In the AI Supercycle, Marathon's framework takes on unique structural characteristics:

### A. Concentrated Oligopoly vs. Fragmented Buildout
- Unlike the 1999–2000 dot-com fiber buildout (where dozens of competing telecom carriers like WorldCom, Global Crossing, and Qwest raised junk debt to lay dark fiber), AI CapEx is concentrated among **four mega-cap balance sheets** (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta).
- These four companies generated over **$500 Billion in combined operating cash flow** in 2026. They are not funding the initial wave primarily through junk bonds; they are funding it by **diverting shareholder cash payouts (buybacks and dividends)** directly into silicon and data centers.

### B. The Speed of Supply-Side Deprecation
- Dark fiber in 2000 had an economic useful life of 20–30 years. Even after WorldCom collapsed, the physical glass remained in the ground, ready to be lit years later for pennies on the dollar.
- **AI GPUs do not have a 25-year shelf-life**. An NVIDIA H100 cluster faces architectural obsolescence within **3 to 4 years** as Blackwell (B200/GB200) and Rubin offer 3x to 5x better energy efficiency per FLOP. 
- **The Capital Cycle implication**: If demand does not monetize within the 3.5-year window, the excess capacity cannot simply sit idle waiting for future demand—it becomes **economically dead silicon** that must be written off before paying for itself.

## 3. The Turning Point Indicator (Marathon Metric)

Under Marathon theory, the peak of the capital cycle can be quantitatively detected when:
1. **CapEx-to-Depreciation Ratio ($\frac{\text{CapEx}}{\text{D\&A}}$) exceeds $2.5\times$** across the aggregate buyer universe.
2. **Gross Capital Formation exceeds end-user cash monetization rate by $>3\times$**.
3. **Hardware supplier order lead times collapse from $>40$ weeks to $<12$ weeks**, signaling that the supply deficit has inverted into an inventory accumulation phase.

When hyperscalers decelerate CapEx growth from **+40% YoY to 0% YoY**, the supplier revenue model experiences an instantaneous mathematical collapse (The Accelerator Principle in macroeconomics).
